## Central claim

3. Therefore, the act of observation must resolve the indefinite superposition into a definite state.
4. The only consistent, empirically validated rule for this resolution is the Born rule: the probability of obtaining a particular state is the squared magnitude of its amplitude.

### Terms and plain-language bridge
* **|ψ⟩ (Psi)**: The quantum state vector. Think of it as a complete recipe listing all possible outcomes for a system and their potential strengths.
* **Superposition**: The state of being in multiple distinct "classical" states at once, like a coin spinning in the air being both heads and tails.
* **Basis state |n⟩**: One of the fundamental, definite outcomes, like the coin landing definitively on heads.
* **Collapse**: The sudden, irreversible transition from the fuzzy superposition (spinning coin) to a single, definite outcome (coin lying flat as heads).
* **Born rule (|c_n|²)**: The rule that calculates the chance of a particular outcome. It takes the "strength" (amplitude) of that outcome in the recipe, squares it, and gives a probability between 0 and 1.
* **Observation/Measurement**: The physical interaction that triggers collapse. It's the act of looking at the coin, which forces it to stop spinning and pick a side.

## Strongest objection and negative controls
*   **Opposing Case (Many-Worlds Interpretation):** MWI posits that collapse is an illusion. Upon observation, the universe branches, and all outcomes occur in separate, non-communicating branches. The definite experience is explained by subjective decoherence, not objective collapse.
*   **Competing Interpretations:** Bohmian mechanics adds hidden variables and a guiding wave, making outcomes deterministic. QBism treats quantum states as subjective beliefs, where "collapse" is simply a Bayesian update of the observer's information.
*   **Test to Prevent False Conclusion:** The "collapse" process has never been directly observed or described within a unified, relativistic quantum field theory. Concluding that collapse is a real, physical, instantaneous event is an interpretation, not an established physical fact. A negative control is to consider if all experimental predictions remain identical under a no-collapse (e.g., MWI+decoherence) model.
