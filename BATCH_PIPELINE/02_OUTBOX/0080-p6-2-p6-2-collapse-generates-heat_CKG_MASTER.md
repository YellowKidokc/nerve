This is a canonical property record (P6.2) from an ontological framework, asserting that the physical process of quantum wavefunction collapse necessarily dissipates heat, governed by Landauer's principle. The one-sentence finding is: Wavefunction collapse generates a minimum quantity of heat proportional to the information gained, with a lower bound of \(kT \ln 2\) per bit.

## Central claim
The exact rule is: For every bit of information gained through wavefunction collapse, the associated heat dissipation \(Q_{\text{collapse}}\) must satisfy \(Q_{\text{collapse}} \ge kT \ln 2\), where \(k\) is Boltzmann's constant and \(T\) is the temperature of the environment.

### Terms and plain-language bridge
*   **Wavefunction Collapse:** The instantaneous "shrinking" of a quantum system's possible states into a single definite state upon measurement. *Analogy:* Like a spinning coin (heads and tails simultaneously) landing as definitely heads when you catch it.
*   **Landauer's Principle:** The physical law stating that erasing one bit of information in a computer must release a minimum amount of heat (\(kT \ln 2\)) into the environment. *Analogy:* Deleting a file from a hard drive inevitably produces a tiny, unavoidable puff of thermal exhaust.
*   **\(Q_{\text{collapse}}\):** The quantity of heat generated specifically by the collapse process.
*   **\(kT \ln 2\):** The minimum "energy price" for a single bit of irreversible information processing, where \(k\) is a fundamental constant (Boltzmann's constant) and \(T\) is temperature.
*   **Irreversibility (A6.3):** The property that the collapse process cannot be undone without additional cost or change. *Analogy:* Breaking an egg is irreversible; you can't return it to its whole state without putting in more work.

## Strongest objection and negative controls
**Objection:** Wavefunction collapse, in many mainstream interpretations (e.g., Everettian many-worlds, Bohmian mechanics), is not an irreversible *physical process* that gains *new information* in the Landauer sense. In many-worlds, all outcomes occur, so no information is "selected" or "erased" from the universal wavefunction. In Bohmian mechanics, the particle always has a definite position; measurement reveals it, but does not necessarily involve an irreversible thermodynamic cost at the fundamental level. The heat observed in experiments could be attributed entirely to the amplification and recording stages in the classical apparatus, not to the quantum collapse itself.

**Counterexample/Test:** Design an idealized, theoretically reversible measurement scheme (e.g., a quantum non-demolition measurement coupled to a perfect quantum memory) that extracts information without the traditional amplification chain. If such a measurement can be performed without generating \(kT \ln 2\) heat per bit, it would falsify the claim that collapse *inherently* generates that heat.

## What survives
Even if collapse is not a fundamental process, the **operational link** between gaining usable, stable classical information from a quantum system and the dissipation of at least \(kT \ln 2\) of heat remains defensible. Any *practical measurement* that yields a definite, recordable outcome for an observer within a single branch of reality appears to require such dissipation. The property can be reframed as a constraint on any physical model that seeks to explain the transition from quantum superposition to a single classical record.

## What this does not establish
This does **not** establish that wavefunction collapse is a real, physical process distinct from unitary evolution—that is an interpretational assumption. It does **not** prove that the heat is generated at the instant of collapse rather than during subsequent amplification and recording. It does **not** provide a mechanism for how the heat is generated or where it is deposited. Finally, it does **not** earn the theological conclusion that consciousness or observation has special thermodynamic properties; the "observation" here is defined physically (A5.1).
