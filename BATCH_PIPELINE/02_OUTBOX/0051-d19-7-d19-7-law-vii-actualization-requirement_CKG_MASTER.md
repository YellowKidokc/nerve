## At a glance
This is a formal axiom (Law VII: Actualization Requirement) from a unified physics/philosophy framework, asserting that potentiality cannot become actual without an external actualizer. It matters because it posits a fundamental ontological constraint with implications for causation, quantum mechanics, and the philosophy of existence. The one-sentence finding is: For any actual entity, there must exist a distinct actualizing entity.

## Central claim
Potentiality cannot self-actualize. Formally: ∀x: Actual(x) → ∃y: Actualizes(y,x) ∧ y ≠ x.

## Best concise argument
1.  An entity is either potential or actual.
2.  If an entity is actual, it must have transitioned from potentiality to actuality.
3.  This transition (actualization) requires a cause or agent.
4.  A potential entity cannot be the cause of its own actualization, as this would require it to be actual to cause the transition, creating a logical contradiction (it must be actual to cause, but is only potential until caused).
5.  Therefore, for any actual entity x, there must exist a distinct entity y that actualizes x.

## System or model
The system operates with a binary ontological state for entities: Potential or Actual. The core relationship is "Actualizes(y, x)," meaning entity y causes entity x to transition from potential to actual. The structure is causal and hierarchical: actualization flows from an actualizer (y) to the actualized (x), with the strict rule that y and x are distinct.
