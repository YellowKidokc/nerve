### Terms and plain-language bridge
* **Collapse:** The instantaneous transition of a quantum system from a spread-out superposition of possibilities to a single definite state upon measurement. *Analogy:* Like a flipped coin settling into "heads" or "tails"—the moment it lands, the "spinning" state is gone.
* **Irreversible process:** A physical or logical operation that cannot be perfectly reversed to restore the exact prior state. *Analogy:* Breaking an egg. You can't un-break it to get the original shell and yolk back.
* **Uncollapse:** The hypothetical reversal of a collapse, restoring the pre-measurement superposition.
* **Framework commitment (`framework_commitment`):** An axiom or postulate accepted as a foundational rule within this specific theoretical system, not necessarily a proven law of nature.
* **Landauer's principle:** The physical principle that erasing one bit of information dissipates a minimum amount of energy as heat, implying irreversibility.
* **Quantum error correction (QEC):** Techniques to protect quantum information from certain types of noise and decoherence, sometimes allowing recovery of a logical state.

## Strongest objection and negative controls
**Objection:** The claim confuses phenomenological irreversibility (due to decoherence and environmental entanglement) with fundamental ontological irreversibility. Many interpretations of quantum mechanics (e.g., Everettian, Bohmian, collapse models with added noise) treat the "irreversible" appearance of collapse as an emergent property of open systems, not a fundamental law. Quantum error correction and protocols like "rewinding" or "retrodiction" challenge the absolute nature of the claim. A strict negative control would be a laboratory demonstration of a unitary operation that perfectly reconstructs a pre-measurement quantum state from a post-measurement record, effectively "uncollapsing" it.

**Competing Interpretations:**
1.  **Everett Interpretation:** No fundamental collapse occurs; apparent collapse is a subjective branching.
2.  **Time-Symmetric Theories (e.g., TSQT):** Measurement can be analyzed symmetrically in time.
3.  **Quantum Darwinism:** Collapse is a practical, information-theoretic irreversibility, not a physical one.
