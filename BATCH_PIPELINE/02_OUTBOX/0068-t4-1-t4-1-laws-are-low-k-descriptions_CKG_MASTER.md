## Central claim
Physical laws are low-Kolmogorov-complexity descriptions of high-complexity data; formally, \( K(Law) << K(Data) \) and a law compresses the data.

## Best concise argument
1.  A fundamental goal of science is to find concise descriptions of complex observations.
2.  Kolmogorov complexity (K) measures the minimal description length of a string or dataset.
3.  A description that is significantly shorter than the data it describes (\( K(Description) << K(Data) \)) is a compression.
4.  Physical laws successfully predict and explain vast ranges of empirical phenomena from a few equations.
5.  Therefore, physical laws are, by function, low-K descriptions that compress high-K data.

## System or model
The model posits two primary entities: **Laws** (concise descriptions, rules, or equations) and **Data** (the complete set of empirical observations or phenomena). The relationship is one of compression: a Law acts as a compact program that generates or predicts the Data. The logical structure is that the existence of a law is contingent on its Kolmogorov complexity being substantially lower than the complexity of the data it describes.

### Terms and plain-language bridge
*   **Kolmogorov Complexity (K)**: The length of the shortest computer program (in a fixed language) needed to produce a given string of data. *Analogy: The most compressed zip file size for a document.*
*   **K(Law) << K(Data)**: The complexity of the law's formulation is *much less than* the complexity of the full set of observations. *Analogy: A recipe (law) is much shorter than a video of every chef ever making the dish (data).*
*   **Compression**: The act of representing information in a more compact form without losing essential content. *Analogy: Turning a long, detailed story into a powerful, short proverb that conveys the same lesson.*

## Strongest objection and negative controls
**Objection**: Many candidate "laws" in complex systems (e.g., in biology, economics) are not low-K; they are lengthy, context-dependent rules or are simply phenomenological fits. The criterion \( K(Law) << K(Data) \) may be a post-hoc property of *physics* laws, not a necessary feature of all natural regularities. Furthermore, K is uncomputable and language-dependent, making the criterion impractical and ambiguous for evaluating real scientific theories.

**Counterexamples / Tests**: 1) Identify a widely accepted scientific law where the simplest known formulation is arguably as long as a raw data dump of its domain (e.g., the Drake Equation, complex climate model parameterizations). 2) Construct a formal system where the most compact description of its behavior is not simpler than its state enumeration.

## What survives
The defensible core is the **framing of physical laws as optimal compressions**. Even if the strict inequality \( K(Law) << K(Data) \) is not universally true or formally provable, the heuristic that powerful laws are remarkably concise descriptions of complex phenomena remains a central pillar of scientific methodology and a productive lens for analyzing theories.

## What this does not establish
This does **not** establish that all regularities in nature are compressible, nor that the universe is inherently simple. It does **not** provide a method to discover laws, as K is uncomputable. It does **not** prove that our current laws are the minimal possible descriptions. Finally, it is a formal/ontological postulate, not an empirical fact; one cannot measure K(Law) and K(Data) to verify it for gravity or quantum mechanics.
