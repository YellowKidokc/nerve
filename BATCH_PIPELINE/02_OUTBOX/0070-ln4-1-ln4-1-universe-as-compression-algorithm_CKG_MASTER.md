**Objection:** The claim confuses map with territory. The universe is a physical system; describing it simply does not mean it *is* a description-generating algorithm. The appearance of simple laws could be an anthropic selection effect or an emergent property of our local spacetime patch, not a fundamental ontological drive. The concept of "self-description" is semantically problematic for a non-linguistic entity.

**Counterexamples:** 1. A chaotic, high-complexity universe is logically possible and not ruled out by this framework. 2. If the universe is performing this minimization, it suggests hypercomputational power, which is physically dubious.

**Competing Interpretations:** 1. **Platonic:** Laws exist abstractly; the universe instantiates them. 2. **Temporal:** Complexity increases over time (second law of thermodynamics), contradicting a drive toward minimal description. 3. **Brute Fact:** The universe just *is*, with no deeper functional explanation.

**Negative Control Test:** Identify a universe-scale phenomenon that actively *increases* descriptive complexity (e.g., star formation, life) and show this is not subsidiary to a broader compression trend.
