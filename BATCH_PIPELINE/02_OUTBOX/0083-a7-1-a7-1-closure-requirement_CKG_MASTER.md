## At a glance
This is a canonical axiom record (A7.1) asserting a framework commitment that physical theories require boundary conditions to achieve closure and avoid underdetermination. It matters because it establishes a necessary condition for a complete physical description, forming a bridge from mathematical physics to ontological and theological reasoning. The one-sentence finding is: A physical theory is underdetermined and incomplete without the specification of boundary conditions.

## Central claim
The exact rule is: For any physical theory, the conjunction of the theory's laws and a complete set of boundary conditions is necessary and sufficient to yield a unique solution; the theory alone is underdetermined.

## Best concise argument
1.  Physical theories are typically expressed as systems of differential equations (e.g., PDEs).
2.  The general solution to such equations contains free parameters or functions.
3.  To select a specific, unique solution corresponding to a physical scenario, additional constraints are required.
4.  These constraints are the boundary (and/or initial) conditions.
5.  Therefore, a physical theory without specified boundary conditions is underdetermined and lacks closure.

## System or model
The system posits a logical relationship between three entities: a **Theory** (a set of physical laws, often differential equations), **Boundary Conditions (BCs)** (constraints on the solution at spatial/temporal limits), and a **Unique Solution** (the specific physical prediction). The structure is causal/logical: `Theory + BCs → unique solution`. The absence of BCs leaves the theory in an underdetermined state.

### Terms and plain-language bridge
*   **Physical Theory:** A set of mathematical laws describing nature (e.g., Einstein's field equations). *Analogy:* The rules of chess.
*   **Boundary Conditions (BCs):** Constraints that specify the state of a system at its edges or beginning/end. *Analogy:* Specifying the starting positions of all chess pieces on the board. The rules alone don't tell you how a specific game starts.
*   **Closure:** The property of a system being fully determined, with no degrees of freedom left unspecified. *Analogy:* Having a complete, unambiguous recipe, not just a list of ingredients.
*   **Underdetermined:** A state where multiple distinct solutions are equally consistent with the given information. *Analogy:* Knowing only "a person lives in a city" – this fits millions of people; you need more data to pinpoint one.
*   **Unique Solution:** The single, specific outcome predicted by the theory when combined with BCs. *Analogy:* The single, final board state of a chess game played from a specific starting position with perfect play.
